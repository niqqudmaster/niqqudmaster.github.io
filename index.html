FAQ

What are niqqud?

Niqqud are the little dots around Hebrew characters. They denote the specific vowel sounds made when pronouncing words and are used in numerous Hebrew dictionaries. While most other services use transliterations, NiqqudMaster uses niqqud to indicate pronunciation, which increases immersion and helps you become proficient in reading Hebrew texts early on.

What's wrong with transliterations?

Transliterations, i.e. representations of Hebrew words in another alphabet, are a sub-optimal approach to studying Hebrew. Not only are they ambiguous and inaccurate as there is no standardised transliteration system, they also fail to contribute to Hebrew reading proficiency. They constitute an inefficient approach to learning pronunciation as you essentially need to process and learn two words instead of one. They can also undermine your learning confidence as they make you rely on a non-native alphabet.

What does it mean when niqqud are gradually vanishing?

Gradually vanishing niqqud is NiqqudMaster's one-of-a-kind two-step approach to learning Hebrew vocabulary. At first all flashcards are displayed with niqqud. After several correct repetitions, the niqqud are dropped, and the word is shown in the form it most frequently appears in in everyday written Hebrew. This approach facilitates an exceptionally smooth and immersive learning experience, which mimics the way Israeli children study Hebrew in elementary school.
